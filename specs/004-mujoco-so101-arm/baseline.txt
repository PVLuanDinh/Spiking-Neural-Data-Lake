PHASE 4 FINDINGS

  1. THE CALIBRATION GATE HAD AN UNENFORCED PRECONDITION, now closed. serial_arm's docstring
     always said allow_uncalibrated was for a bench/echo test "(with a fake writer=)", but
     assert_calibrated only ever received the flag -- so
        SerialArm(port="COM3", allow_uncalibrated=True)
     passed the gate and then opened a REAL pyserial port with placeholder safety limits. That
     is the single path to the servos calibration exists to close. Now enforced in the
     CONSTRUCTOR (not in assert_calibrated, which live_arm.py:220 also calls with no port in
     sight -- putting it there made a bare policy question raise). A legitimate bench test
     already injects a writer, so nothing legitimate broke.

  2. THREE OF MY OWN TESTS WERE WRONG; THE GATE WAS RIGHT. Two drove shoulder_lift to ctrl_LO,
     which MEASURED reaches cleanly with no contact -- it is ctrl_HI that hits the floor
     (commanded +100 deg, achieved +37.80, contact GROUND, blocked True). Wrong sign, not a
     wrong gate. The third asserted EXACT pose equality after 50 HOLDs; a position servo droops
     under gravity, so zero movement was never physically right. Rewritten as STATIONARITY --
     drift must settle, not accumulate -- which keeps the intent (velocity cleared, no runaway)
     and needs no invented threshold.

  3. I MISREAD A PASSING GATE AS A GAP. brain_atlas and the dormancy gate appeared to accept the
     unregistered module; I nearly reported a blind spot. They had caught it -- the summary
     table prints BEFORE the assert, and `Select-Object -Last 6` showed the summary while the
     traceback sat above it. Exit codes, not tail output.

  4. `reached` needed a tolerance and a hardcoded one would violate P1. A position actuator's
     steady-state droop is tau_gravity / kp, and BOTH are read from the model -- so the
     tolerance is derived and tracks a model change instead of going stale.

PHASE 3 FINDING — the benchmark nearly reported the OPPOSITE of the truth.
  The model's actuator_forcerange is 3.350 N*m = 34.1 kg*cm, which is STALL torque (the build
  notes quote ~30 kg*cm stall at 12V for this gear). The first run compared against it and
  reported shoulder_lift at 86% utilisation -- comfortable headroom. Against RATED torque
  (7.5 kg*cm, roughly a quarter of stall, which is the rule the notes state themselves) the
  same load is 3.91x OVER. Three joints exceed continuous rating with 500 g at reach:
  shoulder_lift 3.91x, elbow_flex 4.66x, wrist_flex 2.21x. None exceeds stall -- which is the
  dangerous middle, where a joint moves, heats, and trips thermal cutoff.
  Both columns are now reported and the rated one is marked as the deciding figure.
